## Null case: priority findings

1. **The primary does not isolate the effect of conditioning.** It compares iterative inference with `M1-iter` against one-shot inference with `M1-ctrl` ([protocol lines 13, 30](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/PROTOCOL.md:13>)). That is a comparison of two complete systems: model training condition, inference mode, and number of steps all differ. The exp12 README says the *training pairs* were matched exactly and in the same order, but doesn’t establish that training schedules or every other training condition matched ([README line 4](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp12_matched_control/README.md:4>)).

2. **Input lengths and truncation differ by inference mode.** Iterative queries grow as paragraphs are selected; one-shot queries do not. `encode` truncates the query side to 512 tokens, with a `longest_first` fallback when that fails ([data.py lines 81–91](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/data.py:81>)). Thus later iterative steps can see a truncated question/conditioning history, and candidate passages may be treated differently. The notebook checks the A1 *model weights* hash, but not tokenizer files; it only prints model hashes instead of asserting them ([make_notebook.py lines 27–29, 42–48](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/make_notebook.py:27>)). **Cheap check:** log and compare per-step token lengths, truncation/fallback counts, and actual model/tokenizer hashes before analysis.

3. **Results can silently be stale.** `rank.py` skips any config name already in the output file, without checking its checkpoint, tokenizer, code, split contents, or run identity ([rank.py lines 60–68](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/rank.py:60>)). The notebook uses a stable output directory for the pinned commit ([make_notebook.py lines 16–20](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/make_notebook.py:16>)). **Before running:** use a unique empty results directory; store and assert hashes for each checkpoint and tokenizer; include those hashes and the commit in each rank file; reject duplicates and mismatches.

4. **The “fresh” cascade evaluation overlaps the primary.** All 166 `f4_dev` questions are in `dev_fresh` (confirmed from `split_ids.json`); `f4_train` and `router_train` are disjoint. So cascade secondary results reuse a set already used in the primary, and the claims are statistically dependent. **Cheap check:** report cascade results on the 851 `dev_fresh \ f4_dev` questions as a sensitivity analysis; keep the registered result labeled as overlapping.

5. **The router’s training provenance needs checking.** The protocol calls `router_train` exp10’s holdout but also says the questions were unused for prior selection ([protocol lines 23, 26, 37–40](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/PROTOCOL.md:23>)). The supplied exp12 README does not resolve whether exp10’s holdout influenced prior model or feature decisions. **Before running:** audit prior experiment usage of those IDs; if used for selection, label router performance as non-fresh and reserve a new router-training split.

6. **Primary power is weak for the effect the authors now expect.** The protocol’s 80% MDE is about +0.08, while it expects the selected exp12 estimate (+0.19) to shrink ([protocol lines 7, 36, 66](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/PROTOCOL.md:7>)). A nonsignificant result would not distinguish no useful effect from a smaller positive effect. The exp12 README itself says its overall matched effect was +0.04 and nonsignificant ([README lines 10–18](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp12_matched_control/README.md:10>)). **Before running:** state a minimum worthwhile effect and power for it, or frame this as estimation with the CI as primary evidence.

7. **Pooling train- and dev-sourced 4-hop questions can hide a stratum failure.** The primary pools 400 sampled train questions with all 166 dev questions for one McNemar test; separate stratum differences are reported but are not gates ([protocol lines 21–22, 30–35](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/PROTOCOL.md:21>); [analyze.py lines 57–71](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/analyze.py:57>)). “Both positive” is explicitly only a check. **Before running:** pre-specify the target weighting and a source-stratified estimate/test, with an interaction or consistency rule if claiming a general 4-hop effect.

8. **The non-inferiority rule is fragile at its boundary.** It uses a percentile bootstrap and compares the *rounded-to-4-decimals* lower CI bound to −0.01 ([analyze.py lines 43–45, 99–103](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/analyze.py:43>)). A borderline decision can change through rounding; the 0.01 margin also needs a practical justification. **Before running:** compare unrounded bounds, predefine a one-sided CI procedure and justify the margin in recall points.

9. **Multiplicity is waved through by the claim ledger.** There are three confirmatory claims (primary and two cascade claims), each at 0.05, plus many descriptive recall@k and hop slices ([protocol lines 42–50](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/PROTOCOL.md:42>); [analyze.py lines 91–103](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/analyze.py:91>)). Per-claim error rates do not control the chance of at least one favorable claim. **Before running:** name one primary claim for the experiment; label the others secondary/exploratory or define a familywise adjustment.

10. **The cascade claim may be positive without being a useful cascade.** The router is trained on a 6.3% 4-hop rate and evaluated where 16.3% are 4-hop; class-balanced weights plus threshold 0.5 do not guarantee a useful routing rate or cost ([protocol lines 24, 40](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/PROTOCOL.md:24>); [analyze.py lines 75–96](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/analyze.py:75>)). Cascade-vs-one-shot significance can still accompany poor precision, excessive passes, or no savings over always-iterative. **Cheap check:** predefine a maximum mean-pass budget and report the joint outcome: recall gain, non-inferiority, routed fraction, precision, and passes.

11. **Other checks before interpreting a positive result:** verify ranks are full permutations and gold masks/orders have matching lengths—currently `hits` trusts both ([analyze.py lines 34–35](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/analyze.py:34>)); verify all expected configs exist and there is exactly one per split/config (duplicate lines silently overwrite in `ranks`, [lines 24–31](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/analyze.py:24>)); record actual GPU/model device and inference settings. The notebook requests a T4 and prints GPU info, but does not assert it ([make_notebook.py lines 47–50](</C:/code/qualiusp2026/experiments/exp13_cascade/make_notebook.py:47>)). No hardware mismatch is demonstrated in the reviewed files; it is simply not enforced.
