## Findings so far

1. **Multi-hop structure matters, but cosine thresholds plateau.** The hop gate helps, yet only about 1 in 4 kept paragraphs is gold (exp01).
2. **Reading question and paragraph together helps a bit even untrained.** The zero-shot cross-encoder slightly beats the hop gate (exp02 A0).
2b. **A 22.7M fine-tuned selector closes most of the gap.** It keeps both gold in 91.5% of questions at 391 tokens (oracle 323), about 2.6 paragraphs on average. Its estimated cost is ~1/180 of the generator FLOPs it saves (exp02 A1). Small-data fine-tuning (n200) needs ~2Ã— the tokens for the same recall, which leaves room for exp03.
2d. **Weight editing has little to work with here (exp03 probe).** A0 has no unused linear signal for multi-hop relevance that an editor could unlock; small-data fine-tuning already beats the best linear readout of A0.
2c. **Answer quality holds.** With the paper's generator, every A1 rule matches k=10 F1 (paired CIs include 0) at 68â€“77% fewer tokens. Gold-only context *beats* k=10 by +0.040 F1 (CI excludes 0), so distractors hurt the generator.
3. **The literature ceiling is high.** Large trained selectors pick exactly both gold paragraphs 92â€“97.5% of the time (SAE â†’ Beam Retrieval). Our question is how small and cheap the selector can be.
4. **Embedder mismatch:** the BRACIS Noise-Gate numbers appear to come from OpenAI `text-embedding-3-small` (per the `cart-code` runner), not the MiniLM model stated in the paper. To be checked.

# exp07 protocol: a chain-aware, adaptive evidence selector (pre-registered 2026-11-13)

## Questions (from the PI)

1. Can a small model judge whether two paragraphs **complete each other**, rather than score each alone?
2. Can retrieval be **adaptive**: 2 paragraphs when the evidence pair is clear, 3â€“4 when it is not?

## Design (cheap by construction)

- **No extra cross-encoder passes.** A1 scores the 10 paragraphs once, as today.
- **Pair scorer** over the 6 pairs of A1's top 4, using A1's cached scores and 384-d pooler vectors plus cheap text features:
  - does each paragraph's title appear in the other paragraph?
  - does each title appear in the question?
  - word overlaps, and shared capitalised words
- **Two scorers:**
  - `text`: logistic regression on A1 scores + text features
  - `full`: a 64-unit MLP that also uses the vectors
- **Training data:** the exp05 pool (4,000 unused validation questions; 80% train / 20% early stopping). A1 never trained on these questions.
- **Code:** `chain.py`. Inputs come from `../exp05_failure_anatomy/cache`.

### Policies

| Policy | Paragraphs |
|---|---|
| A1 top-k | 2 / 3 / 4 |
| A1 threshold (exp02 rule) | score â‰¥ t |
| pair_best | the best pair (2) |
| **adaptive** | best pair if p â‰¥ Ï„2; best pair + next A1 paragraph if p â‰¥ Ï„3; otherwise A1 top 4 |

## Pre-registered comparisons

Thresholds are tuned on **dev** and applied **once** to **test** (400).

# exp08 protocol: the exp07 adaptive selector on MuSiQue (pre-registered 2026-11-13)

## Question

**Does exp07's chain-aware, adaptive selection carry over to MuSiQue?** exp07 was positive on HotpotQA: âˆ’57 to âˆ’71 tokens at recall 0.90/0.95, with F1 not significantly changed. MuSiQue is the stronger test:
- 2â€“4 hops instead of 2
- 20 candidate paragraphs instead of 10
- built so that single-hop shortcuts fail

## Data

- **Source:** MuSiQue-Ans v1.0 (`bdsaglam/musique` mirror; files in `data/`, git-ignored).
  - The official test split has no labels, so the labelled dev split (2,417 questions) provides dev and test.
  - Paragraph-level labels: `is_supporting`.
- **dev (1,000) and test (400):** a seeded (seed 0) random, disjoint draw from MuSiQue-Ans dev. Ids are saved to `split_ids.json` before any scoring.
- **pool (4,000):** a seeded random sample of MuSiQue-Ans **train**, used to train the stopping scorers (80% train / 20% early stopping).
- **No MuSiQue data trained A1.** A1 is the HotpotQA-fine-tuned MiniLM-L6 cross-encoder from exp04, used frozen (a transfer setting).

## Scoring (cache)

`score.py`: A1 scores every (question, paragraph) pair once, keeping the score and the 384-d pooler vector, as in exp05. The cache goes to `cache/` (git-ignored).

## Target and metric

- **"Full recall":** *all* supporting paragraphs (2â€“4) are in the selected set.
- **Tokens:** prompt tokens of the exp02/exp07 retrieval prompt (tiktoken `o200k_base`). The selected paragraphs are shown in A1 score order.
- **Paragraph format and prompt:** each paragraph is formatted as in exp02, `"<title>. <text>"`. The prompt is the exp02/BRACIS prompt with one change: "Answer the HotpotQA question" becomes "Answer the question".

## Policies

| Policy | Paragraphs |
|---|---|
| **A1 threshold** (exp02 rule) | score â‰¥ t (at least 1) |
| A1 top-k | fixed k (descriptive) |
| **adaptive** | A1's top k, for the smallest k âˆˆ {2, â€¦, 10} with p_k â‰¥ Ï„; else k = 10 |

**p_k** = the predicted probability that A1's top k contains all supporting paragraphs. Three stopping scorers (the same split of roles as exp07), trained on the pool with one example per (question, k):

| Scorer | Model | Features |
|---|---|---|
| `a1only` | logistic regression | k, A1's top-10 sorted scores, the gap s_k âˆ’ s_{k+1} |
| `text` | logistic regression | `a1only` + chain/link features (below) |
| `full` | 64-unit MLP | `text` + pooled vectors: mean and max of h over the top k, and h of paragraph k+1 |

**Chain/link features**, from titles and text only (no extra model passes):
- number of top-k paragraphs whose title appears in another top-k paragraph
- whether a top-k paragraph mentions the title of a paragraph *outside* the top k (a "dangling hop"), and of paragraph k+1 in particular
- number of top-k titles that appear in the question
- word overlap (Jaccard) between the question and the top-k union, and between the question and paragraph k+1

**Tuning:** Ï„ is chosen on dev (smallest mean tokens with dev recall â‰¥ target), and so is t for the A1 threshold. Both are applied **once** to test.

## Pre-registered comparisons (test, 400)

Targets: full recall **0.90** and **0.95**.

1. **Primary (practical):** `adaptive` (`text` or `full`) vs `A1 threshold`.
   - The paired bootstrap 95% CI of the token difference must lie entirely below 0.
   - The recall difference CI must include 0 or be above it.
   - **Success** = both, for at least one scorer at at least one target. This is the exp07 rule.
2. **Secondary (chain value, pre-registered here; exploratory in exp07):** `adaptive_full` (and `text`) vs `adaptive_a1only`, with the same criteria. **Success** = token CI below 0 with recall not worse, at either target.
3. **Descriptive:**
   - each method's best threshold on test itself (equal recall, the same procedure for all)
   - mean paragraphs sent
   - results by hop count (2 / 3 / 4)
   - A1 top-k recall curve
4. **Follow-up, only if 1 succeeds:** gpt-5.4-mini answer F1 on the 400 test questions.
   - Conditions: the primary winner vs A1 threshold, at both targets (â‰ˆ 1,600 calls).
   - Prompt and F1 as in exp02/exp07. F1 is the max over `answer` and `answer_aliases`.

## Honest priors (stated before any scoring)

- **A1 transfer is uncertain.** A1 never saw MuSiQue, and MuSiQue's distractors are harder (built from the same entities). Recall at small k may be low, and the A1 threshold may need many paragraphs to reach 0.95.
- **More room than on HotpotQA.** 20 paragraphs (~2,000 tokens) with 2â€“4 needed leaves more room for adaptive savings.
- **Chain features may matter more than in exp07.** Later hops are less similar to the question, so link features may help, especially on 3â€“4 hop questions.
- **The gold set is not the only answer path.** A question can sometimes be answered without every supporting paragraph, so recall is a conservative proxy for F1.

# exp09 protocol: iterative (query-updated) reranking on MuSiQue-Ans (pre-registered 2026-11-13)

## Question

exp08 showed that on MuSiQue the **one-shot ranker** is the bottleneck. A1's top 10 holds all supporting paragraphs for only 0.67 of test questions, and its top 5 only 0.35; at the 0.90-tuned A1 threshold, 4-hop recall was 0.67. Later hops do not look like the question.

**Does re-scoring the remaining paragraphs with "question + paragraphs selected so far" put the full chain higher than one-shot scoring does, when both rankers are trained on the same MuSiQue questions?**

## Data (fixed in `split_ids.json` before any training; `split_ids_make.py`)

- **train:** 2,000 MuSiQue-Ans **train** questions (seed 9), disjoint from exp08's pool.
- **dev300:** the first 300 ids of exp08's dev; used only to pick k for the token comparison.
- **test:** exp08's 400 test questions (all of them from MuSiQue-Ans dev).
- Files are read from `../exp08_musique/data/`. The paragraph format is `"<title>. <text>"`, as in exp08.

## Rankers (four arms)

| Arm | Model | Inference |
|---|---|---|
| `A1` | A1 (HotpotQA), frozen | one-shot: score (q, p) once, sort. exp08's cache |
| `A1-iter` | A1, frozen | iterative (below), zero-shot |
| `M1` | A1 fine-tuned on MuSiQue **step-0** examples only | one-shot |
| `M1-iter` | A1 fine-tuned on MuSiQue examples of **all steps** | iterative |

**Training (M1 and M1-iter)**
- **Base:** initialised from A1. Pointwise BCE, AdamW lr 2e-5, 1 epoch, batch 16, max length 512, seed 0, CPU.
- **Example at step t** (t = 0 â€¦ hâˆ’1, with h = the number of hops):
  - query = question + the first t gold paragraphs in decomposition order (`paragraph_support_idx`), most recent first, joined with " [SEP] "
  - positives = **all** gold paragraphs not yet in the query (order-agnostic)
  - negatives = 6 non-gold paragraphs drawn uniformly (seeded per question and step)
  - truncation: the query side only (`only_first`), so the candidate paragraph is never cut
- **Same data for both:** `M1` gets exactly the step-0 examples of `M1-iter` (same questions, same negative draw). Its query is the question alone. `M1-iter` therefore sees more pairs (â‰ˆ hÃ— as many); that is the method, and its training cost is reported.

**Iterative inference (greedy)**
- Step 0 scores every paragraph with the question alone and takes the top one.
- Step s scores the remaining paragraphs with the query (question + the selected paragraphs, most recent first) and takes the top one.
- After **4 greedy steps** (MuSiQue has at most 4 hops), the remaining paragraphs are ordered by their step-4 scores.
- The result is a full ranking of all paragraphs. Cost: â‰ˆ 20 + 19 + 18 + 17 = 74 cross-encoder passes per question (vs 20 for one-shot), reported.

## Metric

- **Full recall@k:** all supporting paragraphs (2â€“4) are in the top k of the ranking.
- **Tokens:** the exp08 prompt with the top k paragraphs (tiktoken `o200k_base`).

## Pre-registered comparisons (test, 400)

2. **Secondary:**
   - a. full recall@5, `M1-iter` vs `A1` (the exp08 ranker), McNemar.
   - b. **Tokens at a recall target:** for each arm, k = the smallest k with dev300 full recall â‰¥ 0.90 (and â‰¥ 0.95), applied to test. Report test recall and mean tokens. Paired bootstrap CI of the token difference `M1-iter` âˆ’ `M1` and `M1-iter` âˆ’ `A1`. The recall CI must include 0 or be above it, as in exp07/08.
3. **Descriptive:**
   - recall@k curves, k = 2 â€¦ 10, all arms
   - results by hop count
   - `A1-iter` (does iteration work zero-shot?)
   - cross-encoder passes per question
   - training time

## Budget rule (fixed now)

A smoke test measures training throughput first. If the projected total CPU time (training both models plus scoring dev300 and test) exceeds 10 h, train on the **first 1,000** of the 2,000 train ids instead. This is recorded either way.

## Honest priors

- **Gain likely.** Iterative retrieval is a known remedy for multi-hop recall (e.g. multi-hop dense retrieval, IRCoT). Here the step-0 ranker is weak, so a single bad first pick limits the greedy chain.
- **M1 alone may close much of the gap.** In-domain fine-tuning is the obvious confound, which is why M1 is the primary comparison, not A1.
- **A1-iter zero-shot may get worse.** A1 never saw long queries.
- **4-hop questions:** the most to gain, but the fewest examples (64 in test).

# exp10 protocol: adaptive stopping on the iterative ranker (pre-registered 2026-11-14)

## Question

# exp11 protocol: is the iteration skill low-rank and localized? (pre-registered 2026-11-14)

## Question

exp06 found that A1's HotpotQA skill (A1 âˆ’ A0) is **low-rank** (rank 4 per matrix recovers 80% of the gain) and sits mostly in **layers L3â€“L4**. exp09 trained two new skills on MuSiQue, both starting from A1:

| Skill | Delta | Base â†’ target | Inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Iteration** (primary) | D_it = M1-iter âˆ’ M1 | M1 â†’ M1-iter | iterative (exp09: 4 greedy steps, tail ordered by the step-3 scores) |
| **Domain** (secondary) | D_dom = M1 âˆ’ A1 | A1 â†’ M1 | one-shot |

1. **Is the iteration skill low-rank, like the HotpotQA skill?**
2. **Does it sit in the same layers (L3â€“L4)?**
3. Same two questions for the domain skill, as a comparison.

**Why it matters:** if chain-following in a 22M cross-encoder is a small, low-rank change in a few middle layers, then:
- the skill has a specific location, which is a mechanistic claim
- it can be shipped as a tiny adapter on a shared model (practical)## Evaluation

- **Sweep:** dev300 (exp09/exp10's dev split), all configurations.
- **Confirmation:** test (exp09's 400). For each family, run `base`, `full` and the dev300-chosen r* once.
- **Metric:** full recall@5 (exp09's primary metric). Recall@k for k âˆˆ {2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 15} and per-question hits are stored.
- **Recovery:** `(R(graft) âˆ’ R(base)) / (R(full) âˆ’ R(base))`, using recall@5.

## Pre-registered readings

**Gate:** if the dev300 gain R(full) âˆ’ R(base) for `it` is below **0.05**, recovery is too noisy to read. In that case reading 1 is **not testable** and is reported descriptively.

1. **Primary, "the iteration skill is low-rank":**
   - r* = the smallest rank with **â‰¥ 80%** recovery on dev300 (the exp06 bar).
   - **Holds if both:**
     - r* â‰¤ **8**
     - on test, the paired-bootstrap 95% CI of recovery for r* has a lower bound **> 0.5**
   - **Fails if** no rank â‰¤ 16 reaches 80% on dev300.
2. **Control:** `it_rand_r4` must recover **less than half** of what `it_r4` recovers on dev300. Otherwise "low-rank" cannot be told apart from "any perturbation of that size".
3. **Localization, "same layers as HotpotQA":** for each family, holds if **L3 and L4** are the two layers with the highest graft-only recovery on dev300. Otherwise the per-layer profile is reported and compared with exp06.
4. **Domain skill:** r* and the localization, as in readings 1 and 3. This is descriptive: a contrast to the iteration skill.

**Tests on test:** for r*, McNemar exact on hit@5 against `base` and against `full`.

## Budget rule

- After the first `it` configuration, project the total time. If it is over **12 h**, drop configurations in this order: `it_r16`, `it_L0`, `it_L5`. Record any drop in `results/budget.json` before continuing.
- `dom` configurations are cheap (one pass of about 20 per question) and are always run.

## Honest priors

- **Low rank:** likely. exp06 found rank 4 for a bigger change (A0 â†’ A1, 10k HotpotQA steps). D_it comes from about 1,100 steps on the same base.
- **Layers:** unclear. Reading a long query ("question [SEP] picked paragraph") could need earlier layers to bind the bridge entity, or the head.
- **Possible failure mode:** M1 run iteratively might already do well (unlike A1-iter, 0.12). That would leave a small gain; the gate covers it.

- **Code:** `chain.py`. Inputs come from `../exp05_failure_anatomy/cache`.

| Policy | Paragraphs |
|---|---|

# exp08 protocol: the exp07 adaptive selector on MuSiQue (pre-registered 2026-11-13)

## Question

**Does exp07's chain-aware, adaptive selection carry over to MuSiQue?** exp07 was positive on HotpotQA: âˆ’57 to âˆ’71 tokens at recall 0.90/0.95, with F1 not significantly changed. MuSiQue is the stronger test:
- 2â€“4 hops instead of 2
- 20 candidate paragraphs instead of 10
- built so that single-hop shortcuts fail

## Data

- **Source:** MuSiQue-Ans v1.0 (`bdsaglam/musique` mirror; files in `data/`, git-ignored).
  - The official test split has no labels, so the labelled dev split (2,417 questions) provides dev and test.
  - Paragraph-level labels: `is_supporting`.
- **dev (1,000) and test (400):** a seeded (seed 0) random, disjoint draw from MuSiQue-Ans dev. Ids are saved to `split_ids.json` before any scoring.
- **pool (4,000):** a seeded random sample of MuSiQue-Ans **train**, used to train the stopping scorers (80% train / 20% early stopping).
- **No MuSiQue data trained A1.** A1 is the HotpotQA-fine-tuned MiniLM-L6 cross-encoder from exp04, used frozen (a transfer setting).

## Scoring (cache)

`score.py`: A1 scores every (question, paragraph) pair once, keeping the score and the 384-d pooler vector, as in exp05. The cache goes to `cache/` (git-ignored).

## Target and metric

- **"Full recall":** *all* supporting paragraphs (2â€“4) are in the selected set.
- **Tokens:** prompt tokens of the exp02/exp07 retrieval prompt (tiktoken `o200k_base`). The selected paragraphs are shown in A1 score order.
- **Paragraph format and prompt:** each paragraph is formatted as in exp02, `"<title>. <text>"`. The prompt is the exp02/BRACIS prompt with one change: "Answer the HotpotQA question" becomes "Answer the question".

## Policies

| Policy | Paragraphs |
|---|---|
| **A1 threshold** (exp02 rule) | score â‰¥ t (at least 1) |
| A1 top-k | fixed k (descriptive) |
| **adaptive** | A1's top k, for the smallest k âˆˆ {2, â€¦, 10} with p_k â‰¥ Ï„; else k = 10 |

**p_k** = the predicted probability that A1's top k contains all supporting paragraphs. Three stopping scorers (the same split of roles as exp07), trained on the pool with one example per (question, k):

| Scorer | Model | Features |
|---|---|---|
| `a1only` | logistic regression | k, A1's top-10 sorted scores, the gap s_k âˆ’ s_{k+1} |
| `text` | logistic regression | `a1only` + chain/link features (below) |
| `full` | 64-unit MLP | `text` + pooled vectors: mean and max of h over the top k, and h of paragraph k+1 |

**Chain/link features**, from titles and text only (no extra model passes):
- number of top-k paragraphs whose title appears in another top-k paragraph
- whether a top-k paragraph mentions the title of a paragraph *outside* the top k (a "dangling hop"), and of paragraph k+1 in particular
- number of top-k titles that appear in the question
- word overlap (Jaccard) between the question and the top-k union, and between the question and paragraph k+1

**Tuning:** Ï„ is chosen on dev (smallest mean tokens with dev recall â‰¥ target), and so is t for the A1 threshold. Both are applied **once** to test.

## Pre-registered comparisons (test, 400)

Targets: full recall **0.90** and **0.95**.

1. **Primary (practical):** `adaptive` (`text` or `full`) vs `A1 threshold`.
   - The paired bootstrap 95% CI of the token difference must lie entirely below 0.
   - The recall difference CI must include 0 or be above it.
   - **Success** = both, for at least one scorer at at least one target. This is the exp07 rule.
2. **Secondary (chain value, pre-registered here; exploratory in exp07):** `adaptive_full` (and `text`) vs `adaptive_a1only`, with the same criteria. **Success** = token CI below 0 with recall not worse, at either target.
3. **Descriptive:**
   - each method's best threshold on test itself (equal recall, the same procedure for all)
   - mean paragraphs sent
   - results by hop count (2 / 3 / 4)
   - A1 top-k recall curve
4. **Follow-up, only if 1 succeeds:** gpt-5.4-mini answer F1 on the 400 test questions.
   - Conditions: the primary winner vs A1 threshold, at both targets (â‰ˆ 1,600 calls).
   - Prompt and F1 as in exp02/exp07. F1 is the max over `answer` and `answer_aliases`.

## Honest priors (stated before any scoring)

- **A1 transfer is uncertain.** A1 never saw MuSiQue, and MuSiQue's distractors are harder (built from the same entities). Recall at small k may be low, and the A1 threshold may need many paragraphs to reach 0.95.
- **More room than on HotpotQA.** 20 paragraphs (~2,000 tokens) with 2â€“4 needed leaves more room for adaptive savings.
- **Chain features may matter more than in exp07.** Later hops are less similar to the question, so link features may help, especially on 3â€“4 hop questions.
- **The gold set is not the only answer path.** A question can sometimes be answered without every supporting paragraph, so recall is a conservative proxy for F1.

# exp09 protocol: iterative (query-updated) reranking on MuSiQue-Ans (pre-registered 2026-11-13)

## Question

exp08 showed that on MuSiQue the **one-shot ranker** is the bottleneck. A1's top 10 holds all supporting paragraphs for only 0.67 of test questions, and its top 5 only 0.35; at the 0.90-tuned A1 threshold, 4-hop recall was 0.67. Later hops do not look like the question.

**Does re-scoring the remaining paragraphs with "question + paragraphs selected so far" put the full chain higher than one-shot scoring does, when both rankers are trained on the same MuSiQue questions?**

## Data (fixed in `split_ids.json` before any training; `split_ids_make.py`)

- **train:** 2,000 MuSiQue-Ans **train** questions (seed 9), disjoint from exp08's pool.
- **dev300:** the first 300 ids of exp08's dev; used only to pick k for the token comparison.
- **test:** exp08's 400 test questions (all of them from MuSiQue-Ans dev).
- Files are read from `../exp08_musique/data/`. The paragraph format is `"<title>. <text>"`, as in exp08.

## Rankers (four arms)

| Arm | Model | Inference |
|---|---|---|
| `A1` | A1 (HotpotQA), frozen | one-shot: score (q, p) once, sort. exp08's cache |
| `A1-iter` | A1, frozen | iterative (below), zero-shot |
| `M1` | A1 fine-tuned on MuSiQue **step-0** examples only | one-shot |
| `M1-iter` | A1 fine-tuned on MuSiQue examples of **all steps** | iterative |

**Training (M1 and M1-iter)**
- **Base:** initialised from A1. Pointwise BCE, AdamW lr 2e-5, 1 epoch, batch 16, max length 512, seed 0, CPU.
- **Example at step t** (t = 0 â€¦ hâˆ’1, with h = the number of hops):
  - query = question + the first t gold paragraphs in decomposition order (`paragraph_support_idx`), most recent first, joined with " [SEP] "
  - positives = **all** gold paragraphs not yet in the query (order-agnostic)
  - negatives = 6 non-gold paragraphs drawn uniformly (seeded per question and step)
  - truncation: the query side only (`only_first`), so the candidate paragraph is never cut
- **Same data for both:** `M1` gets exactly the step-0 examples of `M1-iter` (same questions, same negative draw). Its query is the question alone. `M1-iter` therefore sees more pairs (â‰ˆ hÃ— as many); that is the method, and its training cost is reported.

**Iterative inference (greedy)**
- Step 0 scores every paragraph with the question alone and takes the top one.
- Step s scores the remaining paragraphs with the query (question + the selected paragraphs, most recent first) and takes the top one.
- After **4 greedy steps** (MuSiQue has at most 4 hops), the remaining paragraphs are ordered by their step-4 scores.
- The result is a full ranking of all paragraphs. Cost: â‰ˆ 20 + 19 + 18 + 17 = 74 cross-encoder passes per question (vs 20 for one-shot), reported.

## Metric

- **Full recall@k:** all supporting paragraphs (2â€“4) are in the top k of the ranking.
- **Tokens:** the exp08 prompt with the top k paragraphs (tiktoken `o200k_base`).

## Pre-registered comparisons (test, 400)

1. **Primary (does iteration help, at equal training data?):** full recall@5, `M1-iter` vs `M1`, McNemar exact, two-sided Î± = 0.05. **Success** = M1-iter higher and p < 0.05.
2. **Secondary:**
   - a. full recall@5, `M1-iter` vs `A1` (the exp08 ranker), McNemar.
   - b. **Tokens at a recall target:** for each arm, k = the smallest k with dev300 full recall â‰¥ 0.90 (and â‰¥ 0.95), applied to test. Report test recall and mean tokens. Paired bootstrap CI of the token difference `M1-iter` âˆ’ `M1` and `M1-iter` âˆ’ `A1`. The recall CI must include 0 or be above it, as in exp07/08.
3. **Descriptive:**
   - recall@k curves, k = 2 â€¦ 10, all arms
   - results by hop count
   - `A1-iter` (does iteration work zero-shot?)
   - cross-encoder passes per question
   - training time

## Budget rule (fixed now)

A smoke test measures training throughput first. If the projected total CPU time (training both models plus scoring dev300 and test) exceeds 10 h, train on the **first 1,000** of the 2,000 train ids instead. This is recorded either way.

## Honest priors

- **Gain likely.** Iterative retrieval is a known remedy for multi-hop recall (e.g. multi-hop dense retrieval, IRCoT). Here the step-0 ranker is weak, so a single bad first pick limits the greedy chain.
- **M1 alone may close much of the gap.** In-domain fine-tuning is the obvious confound, which is why M1 is the primary comparison, not A1.
- **A1-iter zero-shot may get worse.** A1 never saw long queries.
- **4-hop questions:** the most to gain, but the fewest examples (64 in test).

## Question

# exp11 protocol: is the iteration skill low-rank and localized? (pre-registered 2026-11-14)

## Question

exp06 found that A1's HotpotQA skill (A1 âˆ’ A0) is **low-rank** (rank 4 per matrix recovers 80% of the gain) and sits mostly in **layers L3â€“L4**. exp09 trained two new skills on MuSiQue, both starting from A1:

| Skill | Delta | Base â†’ target | Inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Iteration** (primary) | D_it = M1-iter âˆ’ M1 | M1 â†’ M1-iter | iterative (exp09: 4 greedy steps, tail ordered by the step-3 scores) |
| **Domain** (secondary) | D_dom = M1 âˆ’ A1 | A1 â†’ M1 | one-shot |

1. **Is the iteration skill low-rank, like the HotpotQA skill?**
2. **Does it sit in the same layers (L3â€“L4)?**
3. Same two questions for the domain skill, as a comparison.

**Why it matters:** if chain-following in a 22M cross-encoder is a small, low-rank change in a few middle layers, then:
- the skill has a specific location, which is a mechanistic claim
- it can be shipped as a tiny adapter on a shared model (practical)

**Known so far (novelty boundary):** that fine-tuning deltas are often low-rank is established (intrinsic dimension, Aghajanyan et al. 2021; LoRA, Hu et al. 2022; task arithmetic, Ilharco et al. 2023). The new part tested here is *which* skill (iteration over a chain vs domain adaptation), *where* it sits, and whether it matches exp06.

**Note on D_it:** M1 and M1-iter were fine-tuned separately from A1, so D_it also contains differences between the two training runs (M1-iter saw about 2.2Ã— more pairs). By construction M1 + D_it = M1-iter exactly, so recovery is well defined. It measures "the change that turns M1 into M1-iter", not a pure iteration direction.

## Method (no data used to choose a graft)

`graft.py`. Each configuration is base + (part of the delta), scored with that family's inference. The models are frozen exp09/exp04 checkpoints.

| Family | Configurations (for both `it_` and `dom_`) |
|---|---|
| **Endpoints** | `base` (for `it`: M1 run **iteratively**), `full` (= target; a sanity check that must reproduce exp09) |
| **Low-rank** (exp06: truncated SVD of each 2-D delta, 1-D deltas in full) | rank 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 |
| **Random control** | random rank-4 matrices, each scaled to the Frobenius norm of the rank-4 SVD part (seed 0) |
| **Localization, graft only** | each layer L0â€“L5 (attention + MLP of that layer only) |

That makes 14 configurations per family. The `it` family is the expensive one: 74 passes per question, about 25 min per configuration on dev300.

## Evaluation

- **Sweep:** dev300 (exp09/exp10's dev split), all configurations.
- **Confirmation:** test (exp09's 400). For each family, run `base`, `full` and the dev300-chosen r* once.
- **Metric:** full recall@5 (exp09's primary metric). Recall@k for k âˆˆ {2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 15} and per-question hits are stored.
- **Recovery:** `(R(graft) âˆ’ R(base)) / (R(full) âˆ’ R(base))`, using recall@5.

## Pre-registered readings

**Gate:** if the dev300 gain R(full) âˆ’ R(base) for `it` is below **0.05**, recovery is too noisy to read. In that case reading 1 is **not testable** and is reported descriptively.

1. **Primary, "the iteration skill is low-rank":**
   - r* = the smallest rank with **â‰¥ 80%** recovery on dev300 (the exp06 bar).
   - **Holds if both:**
     - r* â‰¤ **8**
     - on test, the paired-bootstrap 95% CI of recovery for r* has a lower bound **> 0.5**
   - **Fails if** no rank â‰¤ 16 reaches 80% on dev300.
2. **Control:** `it_rand_r4` must recover **less than half** of what `it_r4` recovers on dev300. Otherwise "low-rank" cannot be told apart from "any perturbation of that size".
3. **Localization, "same layers as HotpotQA":** for each family, holds if **L3 and L4** are the two layers with the highest graft-only recovery on dev300. Otherwise the per-layer profile is reported and compared with exp06.
4. **Domain skill:** r* and the localization, as in readings 1 and 3. This is descriptive: a contrast to the iteration skill.

**Tests on test:** for r*, McNemar exact on hit@5 against `base` and against `full`.

## Budget rule

- After the first `it` configuration, project the total time. If it is over **12 h**, drop configurations in this order: `it_r16`, `it_L0`, `it_L5`. Record any drop in `results/budget.json` before continuing.
- `dom` configurations are cheap (one pass of about 20 per question) and are always run.

## Honest priors

- **Low rank:** likely. exp06 found rank 4 for a bigger change (A0 â†’ A1, 10k HotpotQA steps). D_it comes from about 1,100 steps on the same base.
- **Layers:** unclear. Reading a long query ("question [SEP] picked paragraph") could need earlier layers to bind the bridge entity, or the head.
- **Possible failure mode:** M1 run iteratively might already do well (unlike A1-iter, 0.12). That would leave a small gain; the gate covers it.

# exp07 protocol: a chain-aware, adaptive evidence selector (pre-registered 2026-11-13)

## Questions (from the PI)

1. Can a small model judge whether two paragraphs **complete each other**, rather than score each alone?
2. Can retrieval be **adaptive**: 2 paragraphs when the evidence pair is clear, 3â€“4 when it is not?

## Design (cheap by construction)

- **No extra cross-encoder passes.** A1 scores the 10 paragraphs once, as today.
- **Pair scorer** over the 6 pairs of A1's top 4, using A1's cached scores and 384-d pooler vectors plus cheap text features:
  - does each paragraph's title appear in the other paragraph?
  - does each title appear in the question?
  - word overlaps, and shared capitalised words
- **Two scorers:**
  - `text`: logistic regression on A1 scores + text features
  - `full`: a 64-unit MLP that also uses the vectors
- **Training data:** the exp05 pool (4,000 unused validation questions; 80% train / 20% early stopping). A1 never trained on these questions.
- **Code:** `chain.py`. Inputs come from `../exp05_failure_anatomy/cache`.

### Policies

| Policy | Paragraphs |
|---|---|
| A1 top-k | 2 / 3 / 4 |
| A1 threshold (exp02 rule) | score â‰¥ t |
| pair_best | the best pair (2) |
| **adaptive** | best pair if p â‰¥ Ï„2; best pair + next A1 paragraph if p â‰¥ Ï„3; otherwise A1 top 4 |

## Pre-registered comparisons

Thresholds are tuned on **dev** and applied **once** to **test** (400).

1. **Primary (practical):** at recall targets 0.90 and 0.95 (both gold kept), the test tokens of `adaptive` vs `A1 threshold`.
   - The paired bootstrap 95% CI of the token difference must be entirely below 0.
   - The recall difference CI must include 0 or be above it.
   - **Success** = both, for at least one scorer.
2. **Secondary (multi-hop):** with exactly 2 paragraphs, does `pair_best` keep both gold more often than A1 top-2? Exact McNemar on test, p < 0.05.
3. **Descriptive:** the oracle-on-test frontier (each method's best threshold on test itself, the same procedure for both), and the mean number of paragraphs sent.
4. **Follow-up, only if 1 or 2 succeeds:** gpt-5.4-mini answer F1 on the 400 test questions for the winner vs A1 threshold (exp02 `generate.py` pattern, ~800 calls).

## Honest priors

- **Headroom on HotpotQA distractor is small.** A1 keeps both gold in its top 3 for ~94â€“96% of questions, and the oracle needs 323 tokens vs A1's ~390 at 90%. The best realistic gain is tens of tokens per question.
- **exp05's category A (bridge-linked misses)** tells how many failures a title-link feature could fix.
- **The stronger test of chain awareness** is MuSiQue / 2Wiki (planned next, not part of this protocol).

| **adaptive** | A1's top k, for the smallest k âˆˆ {2, â€¦, 10} with p_k â‰¥ Ï„; else k = 10 |

**p_k** = the predicted probability that A1's top k contains all supporting paragraphs. Three stopping scorers (the same split of roles as exp07), trained on the pool with one example per (question, k):

| Scorer | Model | Features |
|---|---|---|
| `a1only` | logistic regression | k, A1's top-10 sorted scores, the gap s_k âˆ’ s_{k+1} |
| `text` | logistic regression | `a1only` + chain/link features (below) |
| `full` | 64-unit MLP | `text` + pooled vectors: mean and max of h over the top k, and h of paragraph k+1 |

**Chain/link features**, from titles and text only (no extra model passes):
- number of top-k paragraphs whose title appears in another top-k paragraph
- whether a top-k paragraph mentions the title of a paragraph *outside* the top k (a "dangling hop"), and of paragraph k+1 in particular
- number of top-k titles that appear in the question
- word overlap (Jaccard) between the question and the top-k union, and between the question and paragraph k+1

**Tuning:** Ï„ is chosen on dev (smallest mean tokens with dev recall â‰¥ target), and so is t for the A1 threshold. Both are applied **once** to test.

## Pre-registered comparisons (test, 400)

Targets: full recall **0.90** and **0.95**.

1. **Primary (practical):** `adaptive` (`text` or `full`) vs `A1 threshold`.
   - The paired bootstrap 95% CI of the token difference must lie entirely below 0.
   - The recall difference CI must include 0 or be above it.
   - **Success** = both, for at least one scorer at at least one target. This is the exp07 rule.
2. **Secondary (chain value, pre-registered here; exploratory in exp07):** `adaptive_full` (and `text`) vs `adaptive_a1only`, with the same criteria. **Success** = token CI below 0 with recall not worse, at either target.
3. **Descriptive:**
   - each method's best threshold on test itself (equal recall, the same procedure for all)
   - mean paragraphs sent
   - results by hop count (2 / 3 / 4)
   - A1 top-k recall curve
4. **Follow-up, only if 1 succeeds:** gpt-5.4-mini answer F1 on the 400 test questions.
   - Conditions: the primary winner vs A1 threshold, at both targets (â‰ˆ 1,600 calls).
   - Prompt and F1 as in exp02/exp07. F1 is the max over `answer` and `answer_aliases`.

## Honest priors (stated before any scoring)

- **A1 transfer is uncertain.** A1 never saw MuSiQue, and MuSiQue's distractors are harder (built from the same entities). Recall at small k may be low, and the A1 threshold may need many paragraphs to reach 0.95.
- **More room than on HotpotQA.** 20 paragraphs (~2,000 tokens) with 2â€“4 needed leaves more room for adaptive savings.
- **Chain features may matter more than in exp07.** Later hops are less similar to the question, so link features may help, especially on 3â€“4 hop questions.
- **The gold set is not the only answer path.** A question can sometimes be answered without every supporting paragraph, so recall is a conservative proxy for F1.

## Deviations (all recorded before any evaluation)

## 1. Critique: what a reviewer will challenge first

**The strongest story is currently practical, not mechanistic:** a small cross-encoder cuts evidence tokens, and MuSiQue in-domain training improves retrieval. The main risks are overstating what the current experiments identify and treating several exploratory results as confirmatory.

- **exp11 does not isolate an “iteration skill.”** \(D_{it}=M1\text{-iter}-M1\) includes the effect of separate training runs, different training examples and about 2.2× as many pairs—not just conditioning on previously selected paragraphs. The exact endpoint relation \(M1+D_{it}=M1\text{-iter}\) makes graft recovery measurable, but does not make the delta a causal or unique iteration direction.
- **“Rank 2 recovers 95%” is a narrow, denominator-sensitive result.** It is recovery of a particular recall@5 gap on dev300, under the same iterative inference procedure. It is not 95% of overall performance, nor evidence that the model’s general reasoning ability has rank 2. Since the stated full-minus-base gap is 0.29, a 95% recovery corresponds roughly to \(0.727\) vs \(0.74\) on the endpoints given; uncertainty on that ratio matters.
- **There is model and metric selection.** Choosing \(r^*\) on dev300 is defensible if the test confirmation is treated as the sole confirmatory result. But the test subset must stay untouched, and the recovery CI must account for uncertainty in both the numerator and denominator. Test-tuned “equal-recall frontiers” are descriptive oracle comparisons, not evidence of deployable performance.
- **A 300-question sweep is fragile for rank and layer claims.** The absolute recall@5 differences are discrete and correlated across configurations. Report paired question-level uncertainty for all primary contrasts; avoid interpreting small rank-to-rank differences as real without simultaneous or multiplicity-aware intervals.
- **The test data are not official held-out benchmark test sets.** MuSiQue test is sampled from the labelled dev split because official test labels are unavailable. That is transparent, but it limits claims about generalization. Exp08’s primary comparison was correctly labelled “not testable”; its exploratory \(k\le20\) result should stay exploratory.
- **Several comparisons are confounded or underpowered.** Exp09’s M1 vs M1-iter comparison shares question IDs, but not training-pair count or query context; there is one seed. Exp10’s adaptive policy sometimes misses its target on test, so “same recall” is not established by a nonsignificant recall difference. Exp07’s “CI includes zero” is not evidence of equivalence.
- **Recall is a proxy for the stated practical goal.** Missing a gold paragraph can be harmless or fatal; the project already has evidence that evidence omission can hurt answer F1. Make end-to-end answer quality at a predeclared token budget a primary practical endpoint, with recall as a diagnostic.
- **“Low rank” is not “cheaper inference.”** Exp06 correctly says its merged graft saves adapter storage/training parameters, not compute. Per-matrix truncated SVD also defines a particular structured approximation; it is not the global rank of the full model update.

Likely ACL/NeurIPS reviewer objections, in order: **what is novel beyond low-rank adaptation and iterative retrieval; whether the iteration claim is causally identified; whether the main result generalizes beyond one model, seed and labelled validation subsets; whether the token savings preserve answer quality; and whether small gains survive uncertainty and selection.**

The low-rank observation itself is adjacent to established work: intrinsic-dimension analyses of fine-tuning and LoRA already study low-dimensional adaptation, while task arithmetic studies weight differences between fine-tuned models. The defensible novelty is narrower: **the shape and location of deltas associated with this selector’s task and inference regime**, if that survives the controls below. [Aghajanyan et al., 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.568/), [Hu et al., LoRA](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=nZeVKeeFYf9), [Ilharco et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089).

3. **Report spectra and effective-rank summaries per matrix and globally.** For each 2-D weight delta, report singular values, cumulative squared Frobenius energy, stable rank \(\|D\|_F^2/\|D\|_2^2\), and entropy effective rank. Also compare a global, parameter-balanced concatenated update approximation with the current independent per-matrix SVD; otherwise the claim is about the chosen decomposition, not a unique global low-rank skill.
4. **Compare subspaces across deltas.** For compatible matrices in \(D_{it}\), \(D_{dom}\), and HotpotQA’s \(A1-A0\), compute principal angles and normalized overlap for top-\(r\) left and right singular subspaces, by layer and matrix type. 5. **Check whether layer-local directions agree.** Within each layer, compare attention and MLP delta subspaces and measure how much the global rank-2 approximation’s energy and performance comes from each layer. The existing “rank 2 per matrix” could use unrelated directions in every layer; it does **not** establish that two shared directions span the skill across layers.
6. **Add controls tied to the claim.** The random rank-4 control has one seed; use multiple random draws and match each matrix’s Frobenius norm (and, separately, layerwise norms). Also show the unscaled delta norm and the retained norm at each rank. A random control rules out “any perturbation of this size” only to the precision and choices tested.

For principal angles, compare \(U_r\) and \(V_r\) separately: updates to a weight matrix have both input- and output-side structure. Do not concatenate incompatible shapes without a stated normalization and weighting scheme.
