## 1. Critique: what a reviewer will challenge first

**The strongest story is currently practical, not mechanistic:** a small cross-encoder cuts evidence tokens, and MuSiQue in-domain training improves retrieval. The main risks are overstating what the current experiments identify and treating several exploratory results as confirmatory.

- **exp11 does not isolate an “iteration skill.”** \(D_{it}=M1\text{-iter}-M1\) includes the effect of separate training runs, different training examples and about 2.2× as many pairs—not just conditioning on previously selected paragraphs. The exact endpoint relation \(M1+D_{it}=M1\text{-iter}\) makes graft recovery measurable, but does not make the delta a causal or unique iteration direction.
- **“Rank 2 recovers 95%” is a narrow, denominator-sensitive result.** It is recovery of a particular recall@5 gap on dev300, under the same iterative inference procedure. It is not 95% of overall performance, nor evidence that the model’s general reasoning ability has rank 2. Since the stated full-minus-base gap is 0.29, a 95% recovery corresponds roughly to \(0.727\) vs \(0.74\) on the endpoints given; uncertainty on that ratio matters.
- **There is model and metric selection.** Choosing \(r^*\) on dev300 is defensible if the test confirmation is treated as the sole confirmatory result. But the test subset must stay untouched, and the recovery CI must account for uncertainty in both the numerator and denominator. Test-tuned “equal-recall frontiers” are descriptive oracle comparisons, not evidence of deployable performance.
- **A 300-question sweep is fragile for rank and layer claims.** The absolute recall@5 differences are discrete and correlated across configurations. Report paired question-level uncertainty for all primary contrasts; avoid interpreting small rank-to-rank differences as real without simultaneous or multiplicity-aware intervals.
- **The test data are not official held-out benchmark test sets.** MuSiQue test is sampled from the labelled dev split because official test labels are unavailable. That is transparent, but it limits claims about generalization. Exp08’s primary comparison was correctly labelled “not testable”; its exploratory \(k\le20\) result should stay exploratory.
- **Several comparisons are confounded or underpowered.** Exp09’s M1 vs M1-iter comparison shares question IDs, but not training-pair count or query context; there is one seed. Exp10’s adaptive policy sometimes misses its target on test, so “same recall” is not established by a nonsignificant recall difference. Exp07’s “CI includes zero” is not evidence of equivalence.
- **Recall is a proxy for the stated practical goal.** Missing a gold paragraph can be harmless or fatal; the project already has evidence that evidence omission can hurt answer F1. Make end-to-end answer quality at a predeclared token budget a primary practical endpoint, with recall as a diagnostic.
- **“Low rank” is not “cheaper inference.”** Exp06 correctly says its merged graft saves adapter storage/training parameters, not compute. Per-matrix truncated SVD also defines a particular structured approximation; it is not the global rank of the full model update.

Likely ACL/NeurIPS reviewer objections, in order: **what is novel beyond low-rank adaptation and iterative retrieval; whether the iteration claim is causally identified; whether the main result generalizes beyond one model, seed and labelled validation subsets; whether the token savings preserve answer quality; and whether small gains survive uncertainty and selection.**

The low-rank observation itself is adjacent to established work: intrinsic-dimension analyses of fine-tuning and LoRA already study low-dimensional adaptation, while task arithmetic studies weight differences between fine-tuned models. The defensible novelty is narrower: **the shape and location of deltas associated with this selector’s task and inference regime**, if that survives the controls below. [Aghajanyan et al., 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.568/), [Hu et al., LoRA](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=nZeVKeeFYf9), [Ilharco et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089).

## 2. Sharpening the low-rank claim with existing deltas

I’d prioritize these in this order:

1. **Show performance interpolation, not just a selected-rank point.** For each family and rank, plot the full recall@k curve (especially \(k=2,5,10,15\)), plus paired per-question differences from base. Include the full endpoint and report the numerator and denominator of recovery with a paired bootstrap ratio interval. This shows whether rank 2 restores the useful top of the ranking or merely crosses the chosen recall@5 threshold.
2. **Sweep \(\alpha\) on each truncated delta:** \(W(\alpha)=W_{\text{base}}+\alpha D_r\), with \(\alpha\) fixed on dev and evaluated on test. Include \(\alpha=0\), 1, and values around 1 (for example 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25). Smooth, monotone recovery would support a coherent direction; a narrow optimum or reversals suggest endpoint-specific curvature or interactions. This is cheap inference, no training.
3. **Report spectra and effective-rank summaries per matrix and globally.** For each 2-D weight delta, report singular values, cumulative squared Frobenius energy, stable rank \(\|D\|_F^2/\|D\|_2^2\), and entropy effective rank. Explicitly state whether “rank 2” means rank 2 *per matrix* and sum the resulting adapter parameters. Also compare a global, parameter-balanced concatenated update approximation with the current independent per-matrix SVD; otherwise the claim is about the chosen decomposition, not a unique global low-rank skill.
4. **Compare subspaces across deltas.** For compatible matrices in \(D_{it}\), \(D_{dom}\), and HotpotQA’s \(A1-A0\), compute principal angles and normalized overlap for top-\(r\) left and right singular subspaces, by layer and matrix type. Report angles against random-subspace baselines. A small angle between two subspaces means shared update geometry, not shared function; interpret it with the interpolation results.
5. **Check whether layer-local directions agree.** Within each layer, compare attention and MLP delta subspaces and measure how much the global rank-2 approximation’s energy and performance comes from each layer. The existing “rank 2 per matrix” could use unrelated directions in every layer; it does **not** establish that two shared directions span the skill across layers.
6. **Add controls tied to the claim.** The random rank-4 control has one seed; use multiple random draws and match each matrix’s Frobenius norm (and, separately, layerwise norms). Also show the unscaled delta norm and the retained norm at each rank. A random control rules out “any perturbation of this size” only to the precision and choices tested.

For principal angles, compare \(U_r\) and \(V_r\) separately: updates to a weight matrix have both input- and output-side structure. Do not concatenate incompatible shapes without a stated normalization and weighting scheme.

## 4. Process and reporting changes

- **Freeze one primary claim per experiment.** Treat rank, layer, dataset, hop, metric, and alpha sweeps as exploratory unless they have a prespecified hierarchy. Correct or qualify for the many comparisons; report all tried configurations, including budget-triggered omissions.
- **Keep a real final holdout.** Use the existing dev for tuning and a separately reserved, never-inspected question set for confirmation where possible. If the thesis must use labelled MuSiQue-dev, describe it as a sampled held-out subset of dev and do not imply official-test generalization.
- **Use uncertainty for ratios and operating points.** Bootstrap questions as paired units and recompute the full recovery ratio, threshold selection, and target-recall result within each resample. Report the denominator and raw endpoints. For deployment claims, prefer noninferiority margins over “recall CI includes zero.”
- **Separate quality from evidence coverage.** At the chosen token budgets, report answer F1/EM (or the task’s standard answer metric), recall, prompt tokens, and selector inference cost. State the noninferiority margin before evaluation.
- **Replicate training.** At least two seeds for the main M1/M1-iter comparison would show whether the delta’s apparent subspace is stable. For exp11, compare directions across seed-paired deltas before calling them a skill.
- **Document the actual files.** `experiments/exp11_iteration_skill/README.md` was requested but is absent; the exp11 protocol exists and the partial results were provided in your message. I used the protocol plus those partial numbers.

The thesis can make a credible contribution if it separates three things cleanly: **token-efficient selection**, **the incremental value of query-conditioned iteration after matching training exposure**, and **the geometry of the resulting update**. At present the first has the strongest practical evidence; exp11’s mechanistic interpretation needs the most restraint.
